# Branding, Visibility & Sponsorship

Branding = create positive visibility, become the go-to person. Doing the work isn't enough — you have to talk about it too.

## The six moves of branding

**Speak up · Contribute · Influence · Find opportunities · Keep learning · Practice**

1. **Communication skills**: concise and to the point, understand needs of others, push your agenda, influence directions, get others on the same page
2. **Find opportunities**
   - Proactively ask to present to a bigger audience
   - Proactively ask for high-visibility / critical projects
   - Volunteer to help create visibility for others (helping others = your own branding)
3. **Keep learning**: leadership/management training, leadership events
4. **Practice**: leadership opportunities, volunteer roles, public speaking (know your audience)

## Speak-up tactics

- Before any meeting, prepare **one** substantive point or question, and make sure you say it
- Formula for speaking up: point of view → reasoning → impact on the decision. Don't just raise an issue — bring a proposal
- When someone else has a good idea, credit them publicly ("Building on what X said…") — this also puts you in the discussion
- Don't undersell yourself: when describing your own work, drop "just," "small," "simple"

## Opportunities to share your work (goal checklist for the first 6 months in a new environment)

- [ ] One tech talk within the team (a recent project, a design decision, a lesson learned)
- [ ] One cross-team share-out or brown bag
- [ ] One internal doc that gets cited by another team
- [ ] One presentation to senior leadership or a key stakeholder (with your manager present or aware)
- [ ] One external activity: meetup / LinkedIn article / industry event

## Sponsorship

**To advance, you need good sponsors to give feedback.** A mentor gives you advice; a sponsor speaks for you in rooms you're not in.

**Direct manager (your first sponsor)**
- 1:1s are non-negotiable
- Ask for performance feedback every 6-12 weeks
- Ask directly: What do I need to achieve to get promoted?
- Know their deliverables and obstacles, and help solve them

**Other team leaders**
- Set up a 1:1 every 3 months: connect, ask for feedback & advice
- Opener: "I'm new to the team — I'd love 30 min to learn how your team works with ours and what you've seen work well."
- These are the people who can be your +1 in the calibration room

**Finding the right sponsors**
- People who support you + people you admire
- Most important: **similar values** (right fit), not just the most powerful person
- Can be anyone you encounter

**Sponsor map (update every quarter)**
```
Name | Role | Which room they have a voice in | What they know about my work | What I've helped them with | Next touchpoint
```

## Improving yourself (the prerequisite for a sponsor to invest in you)

- Develop soft skills (especially communication / presentation)
- **Earn respect** — domain knowledge, execution, leadership. Results earn respect
- **Be authentic** — follow through on what you say; connect without an agenda (that's what actually builds trust)
- Find a good mentor — someone to help you navigate a complex environment (one outside the company, and one inside who's two levels above you)

## Fitting in (a common gap for engineers whose home workplace culture differs from US norms, for example many immigrant engineers)

- Be willing to take more risks: take on risky projects, fail fast, learn fast
- Don't avoid confrontation: push back on unreasonable requests to earn respect; don't be too nice
- Speak up with substance
- Give credit when credit is due (different from kissing up)
- Fit in with the local culture; support and help each other grow
- Multicultural EQ
