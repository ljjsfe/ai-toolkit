# Managing Up — your relationship with your manager

Core idea: **Manage your manager to manage you.** Your manager isn't there to guess your needs — you manage their expectations of you.

## Four things (working with management)

1. **Understand your boss's goals and needs** — don't assume; give the benefit of the doubt; ask about intent privately and look for a win-win
2. **Provide full transparency, create positive visibility** — keep your manager informed of progress; forward any positive feedback (thank-you notes) you get from others to your manager
3. **Escalate on time** — surface problems early, with a problem statement + impact + proposed solutions, not just dumping the problem on them
4. **Push back on unreasonable requests** — with respect (privately), with reason (give reasoning + alternatives)

## ✅ Clearly understand your manager's deliverables and obstacles

Your manager has their own review too. What is their deliverable, where are they stuck, and which part can you help solve? That's your top priority. The essence of sponsorship: you help them win, they help you get promoted.

## 1:1 rules

- 1:1s don't get canceled — if one is canceled, reschedule it immediately (your manager canceling doesn't mean you don't need it)
- Always come prepared. Fixed structure for every 1:1:

```
1. Last week's done (results-oriented, 1-3 items)
2. This week's plan + my sense of priority order → "Is this the right priority?"  ← must ask
3. Blockers / things that need them to step in
4. Every 6-12 weeks, add: Feedback — "What should I do more / less of to be seen at the next level?"
5. Occasionally: what can I help with on their deliverable?
```

## Alignment-check scripts

- "Before I go deep, I want to confirm — is X the top priority this sprint, or should Y come first?"
- "My understanding is we're optimizing for A over B. Correct me if I'm wrong."
- "I'm planning to approach it this way; any concerns before I start?" (the heads-up you give before taking a risk)

## Escalation template

```
Problem: [one sentence]
Impact: [on whom / which deadline / which client]
Options: A (recommended, with reasoning) / B / C
Ask: I need you to [make a decision / step in / provide resources] by [date]
```

## Umbrella or funnel manager

An umbrella manager absorbs pressure from above; a funnel manager passes all of it down. A
funnel keeps doing it because pushing work to you feels safer to them than pushing back upward.
Change that **safety balance**: privately, at a calm time (a Tuesday, not mid-crisis), make clear
that pushing it to you is now the riskier option *for them*.

> "I've noticed the weekend work has become pretty regular. I want to help you make the case
> upward for realistic timelines, with data. But I have to be honest — my family matters a lot
> to me and I can't keep working weekends long term. I don't want you to commit a date upward
> and then have me unable to deliver it; that would put you in a tough spot. How can we plan
> ahead next time to push back on the unrealistic asks?"

## Creating your own scope

Deliver the core asks fully first — that buys the right to the rest. Then keep a small, bounded
share (Ethan Evans's version: 10 of 800 people, about 1%) on a bet you believe in, tell your
manager it exists, and frame it as upside for them: "I'm using 99% of the team on your goals;
I'd like to keep 1% on this bet." For an IC the 1% is your own time, and only after the core
work is visibly solid.

## Adjusting your message to your manager's style

- Results-oriented: outcome + ask, details in an appendix
- Process-oriented: give more design detail and intermediate checkpoints
- No time: async written updates; use the 1:1 only for things that need their decision
- New manager: ask weekly for the first 30 days, "what does great look like at my level and the next"

## Extra rules for client-facing / cross-functional roles

- Your impact is easily credited to the client or another team: for every milestone, write a 3-line written summary to your manager + their manager, stating "what would not have happened without me"
- Translate client/partner feedback into language your manager can use in calibration (quotes, usage numbers, time saved)
- A happy client ≠ your manager knowing about it.
