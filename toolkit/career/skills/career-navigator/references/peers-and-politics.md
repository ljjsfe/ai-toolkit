# Peers & Office Politics

Where there are people, there is politics. People will use authority/power to push their own agenda — that's the norm, not an exception.

## Top tips

1. Think from other people's standpoints — their **intent & goals**
2. Develop soft skills (EQ, interpersonal)
3. Build good work relationships, maintain a positive outlook
4. Keep it professional (set boundaries, push back, don't overshare)
5. **Speak up for yourself** — don't count on anyone else to speak for you

## Working with peers

- **Understand the intent & goal**: ask, don't guess. "Help me understand what you're trying to get to with this?"
- **Keep it professional**: don't overshare (personal life, complaints about the company, salary, thoughts of leaving — none of it). Set boundaries to avoid being taken advantage of ("I can help with X by Friday; Y I don't have bandwidth for this sprint")
- **Build relationships, maintain a positive outlook**: when someone comes to complain about a third party, don't take sides — respond "Have you shared your concerns with them directly?" Everyone likes working with positive people
- **Face to face over email/text**: don't send anything when you're not calm. Written words are hard to take back.

## Rules for helping others

- Only take it on if you have bandwidth; if not, say so clearly (see the "no" scripts in tough-conversations)
- Give first, then take; favors for other teams should go into your brag doc, and your manager should know about them

## Common scenarios and how to handle them

**Someone takes credit for my work / my work gets attributed to someone else**
- Don't confront it on the spot. Privately, afterward: "I noticed the summary listed X under your name — I want to make sure we're on the same page that I built the Y part. Can we update it?"
- The root cause is usually that you didn't narrate as you went. Prevention: send the update yourself at each milestone to own the narrative.

**A coworker shoots me down in a meeting**
- In the meeting: stand firm + give rationale ("I hear the concern. The reason I went this way is A and B; open to change if we see C.")
- After the meeting: privately ask about their intent. Most of the time, it's not personal.

**Another team wants me to do their work**
- Align with your manager first: "Team X asked for Y. Is that something you want me prioritizing?" — let your manager be the gatekeeper so you don't have to be the bad guy.

**The temptation to overshare (an enthusiastic new coworker)**
- For the first 6 months: only talk about work, hobbies, news. Never share personal judgments or opinions about other people.

## Rules of evidence

- Positive things: either spoken or written is fine (written is actually better — it can be forwarded)
- Negative things: spoken only, never in writing
- When you receive a negative email from someone: don't reply by email — set up a 15-minute call instead
