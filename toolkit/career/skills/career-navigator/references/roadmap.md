# Roadmap — from newcomer to insider

The other modules are tools. This one says which tool, in what order. Most people who stall
skipped a stage: they asked for scope before anyone trusted them, or kept delivering for two
years without ever being seen. Place the user on a stage from the dossier (`dossier.md`), give
the moves for that stage, and check the exit criteria before moving on.

Times are typical for one team at a mid-size or large company; a reorg or a new manager resets
the clock to stage 1.

## Stage 1 — Read the board (first 0–3 months)

**Goal:** know who decides, and have one visible win.

- Draw the power map (`office-politics.md`) and record it in the dossier. Revise it every month;
  your first version is mostly wrong.
- Learn what your manager is measured on and where they are stuck (`managing-up.md`).
- Light-touch contact with three to five people beyond your manager, including at least one
  leader of another team (`branding-and-sponsorship.md`).
- Take one problem that is visible to your manager's manager and finish it. Send the first note
  when it ships.
- Keep a low profile on opinions about people; overshare nothing (office-politics hard rule 1).

**Trap:** picking sides before you can see the sides.
**Move on when:** you can name who decides your rating and promotion, and what each of them
wants; your manager has said out loud that something you did was good.

## Stage 2 — Be trusted (3–12 months)

**Goal:** the people who matter think of you as reliable and useful to them.

- Pre-wire before decisions that touch your work (`office-politics.md`, "Getting inside the
  circle").
- Defend against junk work and take credit for what you ship (`tough-conversations.md`,
  `branding-and-sponsorship.md`).
- Pick three adjectives and run the 90-day perception plan.
- Choose who to back (`office-politics.md`, "Choosing whom to back") and start the favour
  ledger.
- Get the gap list for the next level in writing from your manager (`review-and-promotion.md`).

**Trap:** being the agreeable one (principle 10) — the person who absorbs every bad deal.
**Move on when:** at least one person above your manager knows your work specifically; you have
been consulted before a decision at least once; your manager has agreed a gap list with you.

## Stage 3 — Get a sponsor and scope (12–24 months)

**Goal:** someone in the room argues for you, and you own something that matters.

- Ask a qualified sponsor directly (three conditions, `branding-and-sponsorship.md`).
- Claim scope: the new idea you want to build, the orphaned work after a reorg, the trade for
  cleaning up junk.
- Show up in the next crisis.
- Start saying you want the next level, a year ahead; seed champions six months ahead.
- Take the leverage inventory each quarter; build the weak rows (outside option, replaceability).

**Trap:** asking before the work is visible — it spends the relationship for nothing.
**Move on when:** a sponsor has agreed; you own a piece of scope your manager's manager cares
about; the leverage inventory has no empty row.

## Stage 4 — Run the promotion campaign (the cycle you go up)

**Goal:** the promotion is a decision the room already half-made before calibration.

- Promo packet built from the brag doc, written as next-level work (`review-and-promotion.md`).
- Every voter pre-wired: manager, skip-level, the champions you seeded.
- Leverage held in reserve, not spent: play a one-shot card only if the cycle stalls, and only
  if you would act on it (`tough-conversations.md`, card budget).
- If the manager will not back you after a cycle of delivery, change managers (the veto rule).

**Trap:** spending all your cards before calibration, then having none when it slips.
**After the cycle:** promoted → back to stage 2 at the new level, with a higher bar. Not
promoted → get the reason from the sponsor, update the gap list, and decide with `liu-ayi`
whether this board is still worth playing.
