# Review, Calibration & Promotion

## The process (common across large companies; follow your actual company's rubric for specific mechanics — don't invent them)

```
Peer feedback + self review (pre-work)
  → Manager writes the review
  → Calibration: your manager's manager convenes all direct reports and calibrates ratings by job level
  → Compensation proposal (salary / stock / bonus / promo)
  → Official review released
```

The rating distribution is fixed: roughly 5-15% Needs Improvement / 50-65% Achieves / 15-25% Exceeds. **Exceeds is a quota, not a standard** — you're competing for a slot against people at your level in your org.

Promotion is easy at low levels and gets exponentially harder at each higher level. The bar for N+1 is "already doing N+1 work," not "doing N well."

## Three pillars of promotion

1. **Rating**: a higher rating → a higher chance of promotion
2. **Someone in the calibration room knows you**: when your manager tries to sell you, other people need to be able to +1. This is the real purpose of branding and cross-team collaboration
3. **Level narrative**: every project needs to answer "is this next-level work?" (scope, ambiguity, scope of influence)

## BEFORE the review — things to do on an ongoing basis

**Grow your career with your manager's support**
- Set goals every 6-12 weeks + check in
- Always come prepared to 1:1s; if one gets canceled, reschedule it
- **Compare yourself with people performing at your level but with a higher title** — find that N+1 person and align your work with what they do

**Create positive visibility**
- Proactively ask to present to a bigger audience
- Proactively ask for high-visibility / cross-functional / critical projects (upper management awareness)

**Understand the review criteria (level-specific)**
- Results: SMART goals, deliver on commitment
- Teamwork: help others deliver (peer feedback, good perception)
- Communication: early heads-up, timely escalation, transparency
- Leadership: step up, ownership
- Innovation: be a problem solver, not just someone who raises issues

## How to write a self review (hard formatting rules)

- **No more than two pages**
- **Not a log of activity** ("attended X, did Y") → key accomplishments
- Bullet format, each one: **project → my role → contribution → outcome (a number)**
- Focus on the result
- After writing it, have a mentor or trusted senior review a draft

Ammunition to prepare for each project (your project(s)):
```
Why       Why we did it
What      What was achieved — measurements
Collab    Peer feedback / customer quotes
Quality   Bug count, incident count
Usage     How many times customers ran it / how many users
Scale     Complexity / scalability (especially in a cloud environment)
Level     Which rung of the job ladder this maps to
```

**Provide all the ingredients to help your manager sell your job**: make sure your manager/peers know what you did, customers/stakeholders know what you did, and present to upper management regularly.

## Brag doc (15 minutes a week)

```
Date | What happened | My role | Result/number | Who saw it / who can vouch for it | Which criteria it maps to | Current or next-level work?
```
Trim your self review straight from this during review season.

## Peer feedback (writing it for others)

- The technique: **two strengths + one area for improvement**, each backed with an example
- This is also a branding opportunity — writing something professional, specific, and insightful makes both the person being reviewed and their manager remember you
- When asking someone to write feedback for you: send ahead of time, "Of the X we worked on together this quarter, what did you find most helpful?" to help them remember

## The conversation for requesting a promotion

- Ask first: "What do I need to achieve to get to the next level?" — get a concrete gap list, write it down, revisit it at the next 1:1
- Be aggressive and pushing about it: people who don't bring it up are assumed not to want it
- Track the gap list at every 1:1: "Last time you mentioned X as a gap; here's what I did on it."

## Reminders for client-facing / cross-functional roles

- Impact is easily credited to someone else; every engagement's written retro should state "what would not have happened without me"
- Roles with lots of cross-team opportunity should proactively use that to build support in the calibration room
