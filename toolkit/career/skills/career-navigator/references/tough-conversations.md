# Tough Conversations, Push-back & Estimation — a script library

Principles: **with respect (privately), with reason (reasoning + alternative), feeling not judgment, nothing written or said when you're not calm.**

Before giving the user a script, ask yourself two things: (1) what is the other person's intent? (2) what decision should this message get them to make?

## State feelings, not judgments

- ❌ "You keep ignoring my input."
- ✅ "When the design changed without a heads-up, it made me feel my input wasn't considered. Going forward, can we loop me in before final decisions?"

Structure: **When [specific behavior], it makes me feel [feeling]. What I'd like is [specific request].**

## Push back on unreasonable requests

```
1. Acknowledge   "I understand why this matters for [their goal]."
2. Reason        "Here's my concern: [specific, ideally with a number/risk]."
3. Alternative   "What I can do is A by [date], or B if we drop C."
4. Ask           "Which would you prefer?"
```

Example:
"I get that the client wants this by Friday. My concern is that skipping the eval step means we'd ship without knowing the regression risk — last time that cost us two weeks. I can either ship a scoped-down version Friday with eval, or the full version next Wednesday. Which works better for you?"

**Stand firm when you have good reasons**: if the other person insists, repeat the reasoning once more + "I'll do it your way, but I want to flag the risk in writing so we're aligned" — this is disagree and commit, while also leaving a positive, professional record.

## Two-track estimation

> "If everything goes well, I can finish it in **X** days. If something unexpected happens — [name 1-2 specific things] — I'll need **Y** days."

Purpose: shows you're aggressive (X), while also setting the right expectation (Y) and giving yourself a buffer and a fallback.
**Estimation must come with reasoning**: why that much effort is needed. Break it into 1) known work 2) unknowns/dependencies 3) verification/eval time.

Never give a single number; never say "should be quick."

## Saying "no" / not having bandwidth

"I want to help with this. Right now I'm committed to X and Y through [date]. I can take it on after that, or if it's more urgent than X, I'm happy to check with [manager] on reprioritizing."
→ Hand the priority decision to your manager so you don't have to be the bad guy.

## Opening a hard conversation

- Schedule 15-30 minutes ahead of time, 1:1, spoken (not email)
- Opener: "I'd like to talk through something that's been on my mind — it's about X. I want to make sure we're on the same page."
- Discuss only one issue
- End with one concrete next step + a timeline

## Asking for a promotion / an opportunity

- "I'd like to be considered for [next level] in the next cycle. Based on the ladder, what do you see as the gaps, and what projects would demonstrate that scope?"
- "I heard the Y project is kicking off. I'd like to lead the Z part — I think it's a good fit because [reason], and it would give me the cross-functional exposure we talked about."

Never use "if possible," "maybe," "I was wondering if."

## When you're shot down / criticized

- Don't defend yourself right away: "Thanks for the feedback. Can you give me a specific example so I make sure I understand?"
- Separate fact from interpretation
- Respond 24 hours later if needed

## Receiving a negative email

Don't reply by email. Reply: "Thanks — let's grab 15 min to talk through this." Then resolve it verbally.

## General taboos

- Any writing done while you're not calm
- Publicly contradicting your manager in a group chat / meeting
- Over-apologizing (once is enough)
- Stating the other person's motive as if it were fact
