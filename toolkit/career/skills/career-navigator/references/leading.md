# Leading — leading projects, leading people

To be a good manager, you need to be a good coach. The core signal of the next level up: **influencing people who don't report to you directly**.

## Working with subordinates (also applies to tech leads / startup teams)

1. **Foster trust** — transparency; communicate clearly and uniformly (say the same thing to everyone)
2. **Psychological safety** — encourage healthy debate; dissent carries no penalty
3. **Focus on team goals** — get everyone aligned and excited about the goal
4. **Reward the right people** — reward against clear criteria; favoritism breeds contempt

## Minimal framework for leading a project

```
Why      Why we're doing this (business outcome, one sentence)
What     Measurable goal (a number)
Who      RACI: who decides, who does the work, who's informed
Risks    Top 3 risks + an early signal for each
Comms    Weekly written update — to whom, in what format
Level    Is this L-n work? (think ahead about the promo narrative)
```

## Transparency when delegating

- When assigning work, state the why and the definition of done, not just the what
- Let people estimate their own time (see the two-track estimation in tough-conversations); you only challenge the reasoning
- Fixed-cadence check-ins midway through — don't micromanage

## Visible leadership actions

- Step up to lead, take ownership (the "Leadership" review criterion)
- Set direction / guide / influence
- Volunteer for roles (tech lead, on-call lead, internal share-out organizer, mentor)
- Innovation = being a problem solver, not just raising issues

## Situations with influence but no authority (client teams, cross-team work, dotted-line reports)

- Relies more on credibility (results) + relationships
- Get a stakeholder quote at the end of every engagement (goes into the Collaboration section of your review)
- Make sure the people on the other side get credit too — they'll become your references

## Giving feedback to team members

- Positive: specific, public, timely
- Negative: private, spoken, feeling not judgment: "When X happened, it made me feel Y; what I need is Z"
- Make only one most-important point at a time
