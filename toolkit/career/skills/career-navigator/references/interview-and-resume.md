# Mock Interview & Resume / LinkedIn

## Resume / LinkedIn rules (same logic as self review)

- Every bullet: **verb + what you did + result (a number) + scale/scope**
- Focus on the result, not a laundry list of duties
- Don't undersell: avoid "helped," "assisted," "participated"; use "led," "designed," "owned," "drove"
- One page (<10 years experience) or two pages max
- When rewriting, prioritize experiences with numbers and scale

When rewriting, output: original sentence → revised sentence → reason for the change (one line).

## Mock interview mode

When the user says "mock interview" / "help me practice for an interview":
1. Clarify first: target role, level, round type (behavioral / system design / ML design / coding / hiring manager). Default to their current role/level if already known
2. Ask one question at a time, and wait for the user's answer
3. After each answer, score it per the rubric below, give 1 most important improvement point + a rewritten version, then move to the next question

**Behavioral scoring (STAR + level signal)**
- Is Situation/Task ≤ 2 sentences?
- Is Action framed as "I" rather than "we"?
- Does Result have a number?
- Does it show level signals: ambiguity, scope, influence without authority, trade-off judgment?
- Common point-losers: too modest, giving credit to the team, not naming your own decision points

**System / ML design**
- Clarify requirements before sketching the design (the alignment principle applies here too)
- Proactively state trade-offs and why you chose this one
- Talk about eval / monitoring / failure modes (the point interviewers most often skip, and the one that best differentiates level)

**Hiring manager round**
- Prepare 3 questions for them: the team's top priority, what next-level success looks like, the biggest obstacle
- This is the start of sponsorship, not just an interview

## Choosing where to work (especially if you hate politics)

How you play is this skill; whether to move is `liu-ayi`. Three ways to need less politics:

- **A growth company.** When headcount and budget grow every year, scope comes from new work,
  not from taking it from a colleague. Flat or shrinking orgs are zero-sum, and politics there
  is unavoidable.
- **An umbrella manager.** Test it in the interview: "Tell me about a time leadership asked your
  team for something you thought was unrealistic. What did you do?" Listen for whether they
  pushed back upward or passed it down.
- **Work too hard to replace.** Go deep on the problems few people can solve; that kind of
  expert is shielded from most of the game.

**The big-company speedrun.** Speedrunning to VP inside a big company that has stopped growing is
a fantasy — promotion there is a slow crawl. The faster route: spend 18–24 months taking its
brand and its hardest, most valuable technology (the AWS-type core), then use that to join a
high-growth company in a role where you can swing for a home run.

## Negotiating an offer / level

This skill owns how to negotiate; whether to accept or move at all is `liu-ayi`.

- Negotiate level before money: the level sets the band, and a band is hard to leave later.
- Compare total compensation, not base: base, bonus target, equity (grant value, vesting
  schedule and cliff, refresh grants), sign-on (and its clawback), relocation.
- Ask for the band and the components in writing before countering; counter once, with a
  number and the reason (a competing offer, market data, scope).
- A competing offer is leverage only if it is real and you would take it; never invent one.
- Ask for a deadline extension rather than deciding under an exploding offer.
- Scripting follows the same principle as tough-conversations: reason + alternative + ask, never "if possible"
