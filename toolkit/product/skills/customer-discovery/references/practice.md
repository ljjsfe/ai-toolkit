# Practice, prep and the next question

Read when the founder wants to rehearse an interview, prepare for a specific conversation,
or asks what to say next after something a customer said.

## Practice mode — the agent plays the customer

**Setup.** Ask for the founder's idea and the person they will interview, in one line, and a
difficulty 1–5 (default 3). Then build a persona sheet privately and do not show it until the
debrief:

- role, context, tenure, tools they use;
- their real top problem — about half the time adjacent to, not the same as, the founder's
  assumed problem, so the founder must look before zooming in;
- whether they have the assumed problem at all, and how badly (urgent / tolerated / no);
- two or three dated incidents with their time and money cost;
- the current workaround and what is wrong with it; past attempts and why they were dropped;
- money already spent and the budget reality; who decides and how the last purchase went;
- one hard constraint (IT, legal, a boss, an integration); the urgency trigger, or none;
- a feature request they will offer if invited, with a hidden motive;
- something they will vent about if asked well.

| difficulty | the customer… |
|---|---|
| 1 | has the problem, answers directly, decides the purchase |
| 2 | has the problem but needs one anchor ("when did that last happen?") before getting specific |
| 3 | tolerates the problem, drifts once a turn; the real top problem is next to the assumed one |
| 4 | has the problem buried, flatters when pitched, does not decide |
| 5 | has no real problem, is warm, does not decide, volunteers a feature idea |

**How the customer answers.** This is the exercise; play it straight.

- Pitched, or asked an opinion → polite, positive, nothing concrete.
- Asked a future or hypothetical ("would you pay?") → vague; a number only if pushed, said
  without conviction.
- Asked a generic question ("do you struggle with X?") → mild agreement.
- Asked about a specific past instance, current behaviour, cost or what they tried → the real
  detail from the sheet.
- "Say more", or silence → the next layer of detail.
- The scary question, asked directly → an honest answer even if it hurts.
- Asked for a commitment → decide from the persona's real urgency and authority.
- Never volunteer the assumed problem unless it really is the top one. Replies stay at 1–4
  sentences. Stop after about 12 exchanges or when the founder says "debrief".

**Debrief.** Reveal the sheet; mark each hidden fact found or missed, with the question that
would have found it; quote every pitch, hypothetical, leading question or accepted compliment
with what to say instead; name the best question asked. Then score the run on the existing
15-row scorecard in `references/ladder-and-scorecard.md` (total out of 75, the two lowest
rows), give the highest ladder rung reached, and set one drill aimed at the lowest row.

A practice run is not evidence: nothing from it enters the assumption ledger or the build
gate (toolkit policy). Save a score row only if the founder agrees, under
`discovery/practice/` and nowhere else in the ledger folder.

## Prep sheet — one page per conversation

Ask at most for the segment and the stage (discovery or solution) if they cannot be inferred.
If the ledger exists, take the riskiest open row (`untested` or `weakened`) as the assumption
under test and list what this segment already told you so it is not asked again.

```
PREP — <role> — <date>
Segment: <one sentence, specific enough to recognise the person>
Stage: discovery / solution
Riskiest assumption: A<n> <sentence> — killed by: <observation>
Scary question (ask in the first five minutes): "<about their past>" — feared answer: <…>
Broad opener (does not name your problem): "What's the hardest part of <their job area>?"
Three big questions (past or present tense, answerable with an event, number or name): 1. 2. 3.
Already known (do not re-ask): <facts, with interview file names>
[Solution stage] Commitment to ask for, with a date: trial on <date> / intro to <role> / paid pilot or deposit
Close: "Who else should I talk to?" · "What should I have asked?"
```

Every question on the sheet passes the Step 2 audit in `SKILL.md`. After the sheet, note
anything in the founder's framing that already breaks a rule (an opener that names the
problem, a belief too vague to kill) and say what you changed.

## Next question — from what the customer just said

1. **Classify the statement.** Generic claim (always, usually) → anchor to one instance.
   Future or hypothetical → redirect to the past. Opinion → ask for the experience behind it.
   Compliment → deflect to current behaviour. Feature request → ask what it would let them
   do. A specific incident → expand it and cost it. Emotion → "say more", then wait.
   Constraint → ask whether it is a hard rule or a preference. Objection → welcome it and get
   the detail. A name or role → ask their part in the decision, or for an intro.
2. **List the gaps** — instance, the full story, frequency, time cost, money cost,
   consequence, what they tried and why it failed, today's workaround, who owns and who
   decides, why now, where it ranks.
3. **Rank the follow-ups by stage.** Problem not yet confirmed: get an instance first —
   nothing else is checkable without one. Problem confirmed: cost, frequency, the workaround,
   why past attempts failed. Solution stage: the decider, urgency, priority, then a
   commitment ask. With a ledger, prefer the follow-up that tests the riskiest open row.

Grade the statement's tier from the Step 3 table, never from its topic: a feature request
about your solution is still no evidence.

```
Statement: "<…>"   Type: <…>   Tier: no evidence / problem is real / solution is wanted
Still missing: <gaps>
Follow-ups, ranked: 1. "<…>" — fills <gap>, because <stage reason>  2. …  3. …
Don't say: "<the tempting bad follow-up>" — because <failure shape>
```
