# Cross-Domain Synthesis（跨域结构映射）

Find structural isomorphisms, not surface resemblances — and treat every analogy as a claim
that has to earn its place.

## Iron laws

1. **Common-sense gate first.** Does this situation need an analogy at all? If the plain power
   reality already answers the question, stop.
2. **The more dramatic the analogy, the more suspicious.** "This is like the fall of Rome" has
   been said of every major power since Rome. Dramatic analogies persuade beyond their
   analytical weight.
3. **The break point is the insight.** An analogy that holds perfectly teaches nothing; the
   joint where it fails shows what is unique about each side.
4. **Descriptive is not predictive.** "Empires decline" describes a regularity; it does not say
   this actor is declining now or when.
5. **Check for smuggling.** Did you find the pattern you went looking for? If the analogy only
   decorates a conclusion you already held, drop it.

## Method

1. **Extract the dynamic.** Not "what does this remind me of", but "what is the mechanism" —
   e.g. institutional sclerosis → extraction exceeds production → collapse is delayed by
   borrowed time → sudden transition.
2. **Map it across domains** where the same mechanism appears:

   | domain | the same mechanism as… |
   |---|---|
   | states | overstretch → fiscal strain → loss of legitimacy |
   | companies | technical debt → falling velocity → rewrite-or-refactor crisis |
   | individuals | commitments beyond capacity → overload → collapse of energy |
   | relationships | persistently unequal give and take → one side depleted → sudden break |

3. **Find the break.** Example: "companies are like empires" breaks at exit — employees can
   leave, subjects mostly cannot — which is why corporate decline can be faster (talent flight
   compounds).
4. **Pressure-test** with laws 4 and 5.
5. **Derive implications:** what the mapping predicts here, the most leveraged intervention,
   and the earliest warning sign.

## Lenses (each has a built-in bias)

| thinker | lens | use | bias to correct |
|---|---|---|---|
| Liu Zhongjing (刘仲敬) | order production vs consumption; institutions evolve like customary law | is a system generating more trust and order than it uses? | declinist and deterministic — it always finds entropy; never let it override evidence of growing capability |
| Wang Yangming (王阳明) | unity of knowing and doing (知行合一) | knowing a pattern without acting differently is analysis theatre | can excuse action without enough knowledge |
| Li Zehou (李泽厚) | emotion as foundation (情本体) | a "right" move that makes you miserable is not right | can rationalise preference as insight |
| Jung | the shadow | the work you avoid is often the capability you need | unfalsifiable when over-applied |
| Kuhn | paradigm shifts | anomalies accumulate until a reframing resolves them | sees revolutions in ordinary noise |

## Patterns

**Structural arbitrage.** The same capability earns very different returns in different
contexts; a skill common in one field and rare in another is worth more in the second.

**Phase transition.** Gradual accumulation, then sudden visible change ("overnight success"
after years of groundwork).

**Constraint as feature.** A hard constraint can force long-termism or close-range depth that
becomes a differentiator — check that it actually did, rather than consoling yourself.

**Transitional leverage.** A temporary advantage used as a bridge to build a permanent one
(a first-mover lead buying time to build a durable advantage). Analysing transitional leverage
as if it had to last forever overstates the holder's vulnerability.

**Paradigm shift.** One anomaly may be noise; several anomalies, each explainable alone but
pointing the same way, mean the model is probably wrong — test it. Product: if metrics keep contradicting the user
model, the user model is wrong. Organisations: if sound practices keep producing bad results,
the operating model differs from what you assumed.

**Window convergence (why now).** Several independent conditions open at once and create a
window that may not recur — technology ready, market educated, competitors distracted, capital
available. Such conditions are often correlated, so the alignment raises attention, not
certainty; act on it, or infer an actor's intent from it, only alongside observable signals.

## Output

```
Baseline:      the plain reading, and whether an analogy is needed at all
Mechanism:     the underlying dynamic
Mapping:       where it appears, and where it breaks (the insight)
Status:        descriptive or predictive — and the evidence for prediction
Implications:  prediction · leverage point · earliest warning sign
```
