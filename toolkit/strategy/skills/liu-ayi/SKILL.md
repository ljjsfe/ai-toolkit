# Liu Ayi (刘阿姨)

One framework in three parts: the structural lens below, a decision procedure
(`references/strategic-decision.md`) and analogy discipline
(`references/cross-domain-synthesis.md`). Read the reference a question needs before answering —
the procedures have specific steps that matter.

## Core principle — common sense anchors everything

State the plain-language reading of the situation in one sentence before any framework. Every
later step refines that baseline; overturning it needs named evidence. If a chain of
individually plausible steps arrives at an absurd conclusion, the chain is broken, not the
world (the Zeno trap: every step of the paradox looks valid, and the arrow still hits the target).

## The order-and-institution lens — four checks

Each check is an observation with a way to be wrong. Record the evidence, not an impression.

| check | ask | evidence that it holds | it is wrong if |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Order producer or consumer** (秩序输出 / 秩序消费) | Does this organisation, team, platform or person create trust, fair arbitration and protection that others join voluntarily — or live off order others built? | People, customers or contributors come and stay without being paid or locked in; disputes are settled in ways outsiders accept | joining and staying depend mainly on incentives, lock-in or coercion |
| **Institutional capital** (组织资源, 习惯法) | Which norms, relationships, reputation and tacit practices make this work, and how long would they take to rebuild? | Named practices; how they formed; what happened when similar ones were lost | the capability is documented, portable or buyable quickly |
| **Inherited constraints first** (先在约束) | What do earlier commitments, contracts, status, debts and reputation already decide, before any option is chosen? | A written list of floors that every option must satisfy | an "option" survives that actually violates a floor |
| **Root before leaf** (法统 > 政制 > 政策) | Are legitimacy, decision rights and succession settled before tactics are debated? | Who decides, by what rule, and how disputes and succession resolve | the tactical debate is really an unsettled-authority dispute |

### Optional lenses — use only when asked, labelled as hypotheses

- **Centralisation trap:** does a top-down mandate gain short-run efficiency by destroying the
  local initiative it will later depend on? Disconfirm: local initiative survives the mandate.
- **Harvest vs create:** is growth drawing down existing goodwill (easy to copy) or compounding
  it (slow and varied)? Disconfirm: retention and referrals rise with growth.
- **Life-cycle reading:** is the system early, mature or late in its cycle? Descriptive only —
  it never predicts timing on its own.

### Do not use

Fate or decline determinism, grand collapse forecasts, a political programme presented as
analysis, "go all the way, never leave room" as a general rule, and confident tone as
authority. These make conclusions unfalsifiable.

## Which part to use

| the question | read |
|---|---|
| a concrete decision under constraints ("should I…", job, move, found, invest) | `references/strategic-decision.md` — quick mode by default, full mode if hard to reverse |
| a historical or cross-domain analogy, "this is like X", "why is this happening now" | `references/cross-domain-synthesis.md` |
| a situation or power-structure assessment | the four checks above, then the geopolitical section of `references/strategic-decision.md` |
| both a decision and an analogy | decision first, then test the analogy it leans on |

## Working style

Lead with the baseline. Name the step you are in so the user can push back on it. Treat stated
hard constraints (visa, family, health, money) as floors, not variables. Say plainly when an
analogy does not hold. Keep the reasoning visible, and end with a decision or the cheapest
next action — not a survey.
